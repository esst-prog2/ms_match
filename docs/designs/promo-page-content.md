## Problem Statement

The homework asks for a promo page on GitHub Pages that says what Ms. Match is for, who it is for and how to try it, and that links to the program if it runs online. Ms. Match is a browser-only closet app its author built for their own mornings: it reads the forecast and pins today's outfit up beside a model, with no account and no server. The page has to make a stranger understand it in a few seconds and get them to press **Match me**, without putting anything personal online.

## What Makes This Cool

A Renaissance painting dressed for today's weather. Once the forecast is in (after the visitor answers or declines the location prompt), Botticelli's Flora, in a carved gold frame, is wearing a look picked for the day: closest warmth to the day's high, rain-proof pieces when rain is likely (`suggest()`, `js/app.js:212-237`, run on arrival at `js/app.js:515`). That one picture explains the app: the forecast, the clothes, the frame.

What the code does, so the copy stays true: the look waiting on arrival and each ↻ re-spin of one piece (`renaissance/index.html:310-320`) follow warmth and rain. **Match me** shuffles the closet at random and only keeps to rain-proof pieces when rain is likely (`randomLook()`, `js/app.js:113-121`); it does not look at warmth.

Trying it costs nothing. A first visit opens with a 20-piece demo closet (`js/app.js:14`) and five painted women as models, and the forecast falls back to Budapest when the visitor does not share a location (`js/app.js:169`). A stranger can press Match me ten seconds after landing, with no upload and no sign-up.

3. "How to try it" walks through the demo closet with Flora, no upload. ## The Page Content (draft copy)

The copy below is the step 2 deliverable. Numbers in it must match the screenshots (see Open Questions 1).

### 1. Hero

> *Hero image: the look waiting on arrival, which is the one picked for the forecast (not a Match me result). Caption under the painting, museum-label style:*
> Flora, after Botticelli. Dressed for 16°, overcast, 6% rain.

**Let today's weather pick today's outfit.**

Ms. Match reads the forecast and dresses your model from your own closet: warm enough for the day, rain-proof when rain is likely, each piece pinned up beside the model in a gold frame.

For people who would rather decide in one click than stand in front of the wardrobe.

[**Try it now**](../renaissance/) · [See the code on GitHub](https://github.com/esst-prog2/ms_match)

No sign-up. The live atelier is a demo: it opens with a demo closet and five painted models, so you can press **Match me** straight away. Keep your own photos for your own copy (see How to try it).

### 2. What it's for

*A morning in the atelier*

1. **Check the sky.** Answer the location prompt, or say no and get Budapest's forecast instead: 16°, overcast, 6% rain. Once the forecast is in, Flora is dressed for it, the warmth and the rain worked out from the forecast.
2. **Press Match me for something new.** The closet flicks past and slows down, *Bellissima.* appears over the painting, and a new look settles, up to four pieces, each in its own small gold frame with its name below. On a rainy day it keeps to rain-proof pieces.
3. **Change one piece.** Boots in the laundry? Press ↻ on their card and only the shoes spin again, landing on the pair that best suits the day's warmth and rain.
4. **Read something new.** Under the painting, the daily muse brings a fashion quote, and the stylist's note sums up the day: "Mild day. denim shirt + one warm piece, and you're set."
5. **Wear it.** **Wear it today** saves the look to your outfit calendar, so next month you can still see what you wore on which day.

### 3. Who it's for

I built Ms. Match for myself, and it is for people like me. You would rather decide what to wear in one click than stand in front of the wardrobe. You like opening a page that is beautiful to look at every morning. And you would rather keep photos of your clothes, and of yourself, to yourself.

### 4. Your photos stay with you

There is no account and no server of mine. Your closet, your saved looks and your calendar are kept in your browser, and Ms. Match never sends a photo or anything from your closet anywhere. It makes two outside calls: Open-Meteo, which receives your coordinates (or Budapest's) to look up the forecast, and Google Fonts for the typefaces. Like any website, both see your IP address.

One caveat for the live demo: it runs on `esst-prog2.github.io`, an address shared by other student projects, and browser storage and a location permission belong to the whole address. Those pages could read what you store there. So on the live demo, use the demo closet, and if your browser asks for your location, allow it only this once or say no: Budapest's forecast works just as well. Your real photos belong in your own copy.

### 5. How to try it

1. **Open the live atelier:** [Try it now](../renaissance/). It starts with a demo closet of 20 pieces and five painted women as models, Flora first. No upload needed.
2. **Press Match me,** re-spin a piece, wear the look, then open **Outfit calendar**.
3. **For your own closet, run your own copy** on your computer, where your photos stay on your machine. You need Python 3: download the code from GitHub, run `python -m http.server 8000` in the folder and open `http://localhost:8000/renaissance/`. Under **Add a piece**, add your clothes as photos, each with a category, a warmth (light, mid or warm), whether it is **rain ok**, and whether it is a **dress**; then add a full-length photo of yourself under **Change model** → **+ your photo**.

Prefer polaroids? [The original look](../) is still there.

### 6. Footer

Ms. Match, a project for the Advanced programming course, 2026. [Code on GitHub](https://github.com/esst-prog2/ms_match). Paintings: public domain, via Wikimedia Commons. Clothes photos: Unsplash. Forecast: [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/).
